Lab Purpose & Educational Note: This project operates in a safe, isolated local environment using GoPhish and MailHog. All simulations use fictitious dummy recipients, and the project strictly avoids any real password collection or data harvesting.

Core Phishing Indicators

1. Suspicious Senders

Check the actual email address behind the display name. Attackers use typo-squatted domains or free webmail providers.

2. Urgent Requests

Deadlines like "within 24 hours" or threats of account cancellation are designed to provoke hasty emotional reactions.

3. Deceptive Links

Always hover before clicking. Ensure the true destination URL matches the official legitimate service domain.

4. High-Risk Attachments

Avoid unexpected archives or files with double extensions (e.g. .pdf.exe). Never enable macros on unknown files.

The SLAM Verification Framework

Letter Focus Area Safety Verification Action
S Sender Verify sender domain accuracy and look for disguised addresses.
L Links Hover to preview destination. Avoid clicking unverified links.
A Attachments Verify unexpected attachments via secondary contact before opening.
M Message Watch for grammar errors, urgent demands, or requests for passwords.

Reporting Procedure

How to report suspicious emails:
1. Do Not click any links, open attachments, or reply to the sender.
2. Do Not forward suspicious messages to colleagues (this spreads the risk).
3. Report the email to your organization's official reporting channel (such as the Report Phishing button in your email client or your IT security team's designated contact).
(Note: The address phishing-reports@lab.local mentioned in lab notes is only a simulated placeholder address unless a real lab mailbox has been configured. Always use your organization's official reporting channel).
4. If you accidentally clicked a suspicious link: Report it promptly to the IT/security team and follow their instructions. Disconnect your computer from the network if advised, and change your password from an alternate, clean device.

Quick Self-Check Quiz (Viva & Discussion)

1. What does SLAM stand for?
Answer: Sender, Links, Attachments, and Message.
2. What should someone do after accidentally clicking a suspicious link?
Answer: Report it promptly to the IT/security team and follow their instructions.
3. An email from "IT Helpdesk" states your password expires in 2 hours and links to an external login page. What should you do?
Answer: Do not click. Open your web browser manually, go directly to your official IT portal, or call the IT desk directly to verify.
4. Why do attackers use urgent deadlines in phishing emails?
Answer: To induce panic and bypass the victim's rational critical thinking.
5. Can a safe-looking .pdf file actually be malicious?
Answer: Yes, through double extensions (e.g. invoice.pdf.exe) or embedded malicious links and PDF exploits.
